FEES101 PHASE 00 — FOUNDATIONS
Program Accent study

The system, settled.

Signal red carries the brand and the structure. Green marks money that arrived. Ochre marks what still needs a human. Everything else — around ninety per cent of every screen — is ink on paper. This is the reference all 35 surfaces get built against; one decision is still open on it, the mark.

LOCKED — COLOUR ROLES

Three accents, one job each.

The current build's failure is that every hue is used both semantically and decoratively, so none of them mean anything. Here a colour has exactly one job — and the never column is as binding as the first.

Signal red

#EC3013 · text #AE1800

CARRIES

The mark. Section markers and emphasis rules. Collection bars. Destructive confirmation. Anything that is structurally Fees101 rather than a data state.

NEVER

A row status. The default button. Small text at pure #EC3013 — under 20px it drops to #AE1800, which clears 6.4:1.

Ledger green

#0A6B3D · on ink #35C483

CARRIES

Money that actually arrived: amounts paid, settled rows, collected totals, receipts, a successful reconciliation.

NEVER

A button, a link, a nav highlight, toast chrome. It may fill chart geometry — but only where that geometry is received money, never a rate or a pass/fail threshold. Green where no money arrived means the system has broken.

Follow-up ochre

#B45F06 · text #8A4805

CARRIES

States awaiting a human: partial, overdue, not sent, needs resend, pending approval. Always with weight, and flagged rows sort to the top.

NEVER

A heading, a rule, or a total. Ochre appears only in the status column and in the arrears segment of a bar.

Ink & paper

#201E1D → #F3F2F2

CARRIES

All type, all rules, all primary buttons, all chart geometry. Ink primaries are the single choice that keeps this from reading like a dashboard template.

FLOOR

Secondary text stops at #605D5D (5.8:1). #7D7979 and lighter are for rules and disabled states only — never text.

LOCKED — THE MARK · A, THE STACK

Three candidates, built from the numerals.

The name stays; the rounded mint "F1" square goes — it reads like a starter-kit avatar, carries no idea, and dies below 24px. Each candidate is type and rules only: one colour, either ground, legible at 16px in a browser tab and at poster size.

C THE COUNTER
101 FEES
101 at 16px — favicon

A solid red block — the only filled shape in the entire system, which is exactly why it reads as a mark. Shrinks best, works unchanged on the ink ground, and makes the strongest app icon.

My pick is A — the only one whose logic propagates into the interface rather than sitting on top of it. C is the better badge if you want something that survives as a favicon, an invoice footer and an app icon. B I'd avoid: baking the palette into the wordmark means any future palette change breaks the logo.

TYPE

Archivo, five tiers, tabular money.

One family, three weights. Manrope goes: it's a friendly geometric sans that softens precisely the thing this product should feel exact about. Every figure sets in tabular numerals so columns align and count-ups don't jitter.
